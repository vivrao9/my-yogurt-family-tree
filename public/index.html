Mapping my yogurt family tree

Last updated April 1, 2026

When tens of millions of people live together for generations, they develop a culture. Bacteria is no different.

Yogurt is formed when bacteria like Lactobacillus bulgaricus and Streptococcus thermophilus are added to milk. These bacteria eat the lactose in milk and convert it into lactic acid. Lactose is technically a sugar (ending in -ose), which explains why yogurt is more sour than milk. In fact, Ancient Greeks called yogurt oxygala, which means “sour milk.”

The work "yogurt" derives from the Old Turkish root yog, which means "to intensify" or "to condense." It is believed yogurt was first eaten in present-day Turkey about 7,000 years ago. Most Indians call a similar dairy product dahi, which comes from the Sanskrit word dadhi, which is the land where rivers of milk and curd1 flowed.

In fact, from thayir sadham to shrikhand, we Indians love our yogurt. My parents would tell me that, when you moved houses in India, you would ask your neighbor for a starter of yogurt—because everything has to start somewhere.

So in 2025, I started making my own yogurt. I used my Instant Pot and the no-boil method to make a batch every few weeks. I started with store-bought yogurt and cultivated it. There were times when my batch would develop mold, or not thicken, but I'd try again with another starter (either homemade or store-bought).
